# 📝 EXAM PRACTICE QUESTIONS (WITH ANSWERS)

---

## SECTION A — Short Concept Questions

### Q1. What is the difference between a population and a sample?

**Answer:**
A population contains all observations of interest, while a sample is a subset drawn from the population.

---

### Q2. What is a sampling distribution?

**Answer:**
A sampling distribution is the distribution of a statistic (such as the sample mean) computed from many repeated samples.

---

### Q3. Define standard error.

**Answer:**
Standard error measures the variability of the sample mean across repeated samples and equals σ/√n.

---

### Q4. What does the Central Limit Theorem state?

**Answer:**
As sample size increases, the sampling distribution of the mean becomes approximately normal, regardless of the original data distribution.

---

### Q5. When should the median be preferred over the mean?

**Answer:**
When the data is skewed or contains outliers.

---

## SECTION B — Interpretation Questions

### Q6. A histogram is strongly right-skewed. What does this imply about mean and median?

**Answer:**
The mean is greater than the median due to the long right tail.

---

### Q7. Why does standard deviation of sample means decrease as sample size increases?

**Answer:**
Averaging more observations reduces randomness, following the √n rule.

---

### Q8. Are outliers always data errors? Explain.

**Answer:**
No. Outliers can represent legitimate extreme values, especially in real-world data such as network traffic or housing prices.

---

### Q9. What does a box plot show?

**Answer:**
It shows the median, interquartile range, spread, and potential outliers of a distribution.

---

## SECTION C — Probability & Bayes

### Q10. Define conditional probability.

**Answer:**
Conditional probability measures the probability of an event occurring given that another event has occurred.

---

### Q11. Write Bayes’ Theorem.

**Answer:**
[
P(A|B)=\frac{P(B|A)P(A)}{P(B)}
]

---

### Q12. Explain Bayes’ Theorem in words.

**Answer:**
It updates the probability of an event based on new evidence.

---

## SECTION D — Binomial & Normal Approximation

### Q13. When is a binomial model appropriate?

**Answer:**
When there are a fixed number of independent trials, each with two outcomes and constant probability.

---

### Q14. When can a normal distribution approximate a binomial distribution?

**Answer:**
When both np ≥ 10 and n(1−p) ≥ 10.

---

### Q15. Why does the normal approximation fail for small samples?

**Answer:**
Because the distribution is discrete and skewed, not smooth or symmetric.

---

## SECTION E — Sampling Distribution of the Mean

### Q16. What happens to the sampling distribution as n increases?

**Answer:**
It becomes more normal and less variable.

---

### Q17. Compare the distribution of raw data to the sampling distribution of the mean.

**Answer:**
Raw data may be skewed and wide, while the sampling distribution is smoother and narrower.

---

### Q18. Why is the mean of sample means close to the population mean?

**Answer:**
Because the sample mean is an unbiased estimator of the population mean.

---

## SECTION F — Sampling Distribution of a Proportion

### Q19. What is a sample proportion?

**Answer:**
The fraction of observations in a sample that satisfy a given condition.

---

### Q20. What is the standard deviation of a sample proportion?

**Answer:**
$[
\sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{n}}
]$

---

### Q21. When does the normal approximation for proportions work well?

**Answer:**
When both expected successes and failures are at least 10.

---

## SECTION G — Numerical Reasoning

### Q22. If σ = 100 and n = 25, what is the standard error?

**Answer:**
$[
SE = 100/\sqrt{25} = 20
]$

---

### Q23. If sample size increases from 100 to 400, how does SE change?

**Answer:**
It is halved, because √400 = 2√100.

---

## SECTION H — Explain in One Sentence (EXAM FAVORITE)

### Q24. Explain why larger samples give more reliable estimates.

**Answer:**
Larger samples reduce random variation by averaging more observations.

---

### Q25. Complete the statement:

**“As sample size increases, the sampling distribution of the mean…”**

**Answer:**
“…becomes more normal and less variable due to the Central Limit Theorem.”

---

## SECTION I — True / False (WITH EXPLANATION)

### Q26. Correlation implies causation.

**Answer:** False — correlation only measures association.

---

### Q27. The sampling distribution of the mean always has the same shape as the population.

**Answer:** False — it becomes normal as n increases.

---

### Q28. Standard error measures variability in raw data.

**Answer:** False — it measures variability of the sample mean.

---

## 🧠 FINAL EXAM SURVIVAL STRATEGY

If stuck, write this:

> As sample size increases, variability decreases and distributions become more normal due to averaging effects described by the Central Limit Theorem.

# 📘 EXAM-STYLE QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

---

## Q1. What is a confidence interval?

**Answer:**
A confidence interval is a range of values that likely contains the true population parameter.

---

## Q2. What does a 95% confidence interval mean?

**Answer:**
It means that 95 out of 100 such intervals will contain the true value.

---

## Q3. Define standard error (SE).

**Answer:**
Standard error measures how uncertain an estimate (like the mean) is.

---

## Q4. What happens to SE and MOE when sample size increases?

**Answer:**
Both SE and MOE decrease as sample size increases.

---

## Q5. Given CI = [1.2, 2.5], what is the conclusion?

**Answer:**
The interval is positive, so Group 1 has a higher mean than Group 0.

---

## Q6. Given CI = [-0.6, -0.2], what is the conclusion?

**Answer:**
The interval is negative, so Group 1 has a lower mean than Group 0.

---

## Q7. Given CI = [-0.01, 0.03], what is the conclusion?

**Answer:**
The interval includes zero, so there is no clear difference.

---

## Q8. What is the null hypothesis (H0)?

**Answer:**
H0 is the default assumption about the population parameter.

---

## Q9. What is the alternative hypothesis (H1)?

**Answer:**
H1 is the claim we want to test using data.

---

## Q10. When do you use a one-sided test?

**Answer:**
When the hypothesis specifies a direction (greater than or less than).

---

## Q11. When do you use a two-sided test?

**Answer:**
When testing whether a parameter is different from a value.

---

## Q12. What does alpha (α = 0.05) represent?

**Answer:**
The maximum allowed probability of rejecting a true null hypothesis.

---

## Q13. What is a p-value?

**Answer:**
The probability of observing the data if the null hypothesis is true.

---

## Q14. What does “reject H0” mean?

**Answer:**
There is strong evidence against the null hypothesis.

---

## Q15. What does “fail to reject H0” mean?

**Answer:**
There is not enough evidence to reject the null hypothesis.

---

## Q16. If p-value = 0.03 and α = 0.05, what is the decision?

**Answer:**
Reject the null hypothesis.

---

## Q17. If p-value = 0.40 and α = 0.05, what is the decision?

**Answer:**
Fail to reject the null hypothesis.

---

## Q18. When do you use a z-test?

**Answer:**
When the sample size is large (n ≥ 30).

---

## Q19. When do you use a t-test?

**Answer:**
When the sample size is small (n < 30).

---

## Q20. What are degrees of freedom (df)?

**Answer:**
Degrees of freedom indicate how many values can vary freely.

---

## Q21. What is df for a one-sample t-test with n = 20?

**Answer:**
df = 19.

---

## Q22. Write the formula for margin of error.

**Answer:**
MOE = critical value × standard error.

---

## Q23. Write the formula for a confidence interval.

**Answer:**
Estimate ± margin of error.

---

## Q24. What does MOE ∝ 1/√n mean?

**Answer:**
As sample size increases, margin of error decreases.

---

## Q25. What is sampling variability?

**Answer:**
The natural variation in results from different random samples.

---

## Q26. Why do we use t-distribution for small samples?

**Answer:**
Because small samples have more uncertainty.

---

## Q27. State a conclusion for CI = [-0.4, -0.1].

**Answer:**
The data suggest Group 1 has a lower mean than Group 0.

---

## Q28. State a conclusion for CI = [0.1, 0.6].

**Answer:**
The data suggest Group 1 has a higher mean than Group 0.

---

## Q29. State a conclusion for CI = [-0.05, 0.08].

**Answer:**
The data do not show a clear difference between groups.

---

## Q30. Write a standard exam conclusion sentence.

**Answer:**
“At the 5% significance level, we reject (or fail to reject) the null hypothesis based on the evidence.”
